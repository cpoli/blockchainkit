Payment and state channels
--------------------------

Paying off chain with the chain as judge: one-way and duplex channels, revocation and penalties, general state channels, eltoo, and watchtowers.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Paying for a stream by the second on chain costs a transaction, and a fee, per payment. Jeremy Spilman&#x27;s channel needs two. The payer locks the channel&#x27;s capacity in a 2-of-2 output, after the payee has signed a refund that returns everything to the payer at a lock time. Each payment is a new transaction from that output, paying the payee a little more; the payer signs it and hands it over, and the payee countersigns and publishes only the last one. Mike Hearn built the scheme into bitcoinj the same year.">

Each party holds its own commitment transaction, signed by the other. It pays the counterparty at once, but its holder only after a delay, during which anyone with the revocation key can take the holder&#x27;s output instead. Moving to a new state, each party reveals the secret behind its old commitment&#x27;s revocation key. Publishing a revoked commitment then forfeits the whole channel to a counterparty that is watching.">

Every state is an update transaction, which can spend the funding output or any earlier update, and a settlement, which pays that state&#x27;s balances after a delay. An old update on chain is simply overwritten by a newer one, and only the last update ever settles.">

A watchtower watches for it. After each update the party gives the tower a hint, half the revoked commitment&#x27;s txid, and a presigned penalty encrypted under the whole txid; the tower learns nothing until that commitment appears, and can then publish only a penalty that pays the party.">
